## THE SHELF INSIDE THE MACHINE

*You spent decades getting onto the shelf. The shelf just moved.*

**Theme:** business

**Source idea:** WARC article, April 2026 — AI platforms forging new consumer shopping habits. 1 in 4 UK consumers prepared to ask AI chatbots for product recommendations. ChatGPT referrals converting at 11.4%, above paid search (9.3%), email (4.6%), social (3.7%). 2 in 3 AI-using grocery shoppers switch brands mid-aisle. 75% of near-$200bn retail media spend goes to on-site search formats now at structural risk.

**The ONE idea:** The most valuable shelf in retail is now inside an AI's answer, and most brands have no idea they're not on it.

---

**01 — THE HOOK [0:00 – 0:35]**

She's standing in the cereal aisle. Oatibix in one hand, phone in the other. She types: *which of these is actually good for me?*

[PAUSE]

She doesn't read the back of the box. She reads the answer. And the answer doesn't mention the brand her mother bought for thirty years.

She puts the box back. She picks up something else.

> **SLIDE:** *A supermarket aisle, shot from floor level, looking up at fluorescent lights, one hand holding a phone, the screen glowing.*

---

**02 — THE PAIN POINT [0:35 – 1:45]**

Every major consumer brand spent the last decade fighting for two things. Eye-level shelf placement. Top of Google.

Both are going obsolete at the same moment.

According to SimilarWeb, ChatGPT now refers shoppers to retail sites at a conversion rate of 11.4%. Higher than paid search. Higher than email. Higher than social. And it's climbing 5 points year on year.

Meanwhile, Publicis found that 2 in 3 AI-using grocery shoppers switch brands mid-aisle — phone in hand — right in front of your product.

[BEAT]

You paid for the shelf. You paid for the placement. You funded the campaign that got her into the store.

And she left with someone else.

> **SLIDE:** *Large white "11.4%" on black. Below, small grey: "ChatGPT retail referral conversion rate, June 2025."*

---

**03 — THE SURPRISE [1:45 – 2:45]**

Here's what most brands are missing.

This isn't a search problem. It's not an algorithm update you wait out. The model isn't ranking your content. It's synthesising a recommendation from everything it has ever ingested about your category — and handing it to a buyer who trusts it more than your ad.

[PAUSE]

The new shelf is inside the answer. You either get mentioned or you don't.

[DROP]

You're not losing to a competitor. You're losing to a conversation you weren't part of.

[PAUSE]

> **SLIDE:** *Black screen.*

---

**04 — THE RUSH OF DOING NOTHING [2:45 – 3:30]**

Every part of you wants to fix this right now. Brief the agency. Update the website. Ask someone what GEO stands for.

[SLOW]

Stop.

The brands that win this aren't the fastest movers. They're the ones that understand what the model actually values: earned trust, consistent product information, real reviews, clear truth about what the thing does.

You can't buy your way into the answer. You have to deserve to be there.

> **SLIDE:** *Single blank white slide. No text.*

---

**05 — THE PATH [3:30 – 4:15]**

3 moves. Starting this week.

Ask the AI about your brand. Type your category into ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude. Read the answer the way a customer would — cold, no context, no loyalty.

Build for the model, not the algorithm. Structured product data, earned press, third-party reviews. The model reads everything. Feed it truth.

Stop optimising for impressions. Start optimising to be cited. An AI doesn't click your ad. It reads your reputation.

> **SLIDE:** *White slide. Three lines, left-aligned: "1. Ask the AI. 2. Feed it truth. 3. Build to be cited."*

---

**06 — THE CLOSE [4:15 – 4:35]**

She's still in the aisle. Phone back in her pocket. New box in her hand.

The brand she chose didn't have a bigger budget. It had a clearer answer.

[HOLD]

> **SLIDE:** *The same floor-level aisle shot. Phone dark in her pocket. Different box in her hand.*

---

> **CLOSING LINE:** *"The shelf didn't disappear. It just moved somewhere most brands aren't looking."*

---

**PERFORMANCE NOTES**
1. The opening scene lands only if you slow down. Say "She puts the box back" like it physically hurts.
2. In segment 02, let the silence after "And she left with someone else" sit for a full 3 seconds. Don't save the audience from that.
3. Segment 03's social currency line — "You're not losing to a competitor. You're losing to a conversation you weren't part of" — goes [DROP] then slow. Almost a whisper.
4. Segment 04 is the quietest moment in the talk. Step toward the audience. Drop volume. "You can't buy your way into the answer" should feel like a confession, not a verdict.
5. The close: say the last line, step back, hold. Do not smile. Do not nod. Let it land.
