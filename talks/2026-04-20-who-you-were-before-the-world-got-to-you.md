## WHO YOU WERE BEFORE THE WORLD GOT TO YOU

*The self isn't hiding. It's buried. And the question was never discovery — it was subtraction.*

**Theme:** philosophy

**Source idea:** Who am I before the world tells me who to be

---

**01 — THE HOOK [0:00 – 0:35]**

In 1998, a researcher at the Walters Art Museum in Baltimore put a thirteenth-century prayer book under ultraviolet light. The prayer book was worth almost nothing. What happened next was worth everything. Because bleeding through the ink, from underneath eight hundred years of a monk's handwriting, came something else. Archimedes' own words. His proofs. His diagrams. Thought lost to history. The monks had scraped the original parchment clean and written over it. They needed the vellum. They didn't need Archimedes. The book scholars called a palimpsest — a manuscript erased and rewritten — had been carrying the original all along. Just buried.

> **SLIDE:** *Close-up of the Archimedes palimpsest under ultraviolet light. Ancient Greek text glowing gold beneath medieval Latin script. No title. No caption.*

---

**02 — THE PAIN POINT [0:35 – 1:10]**

You are a palimpsest. Every expectation written over you by a parent who needed you to be safe. Every role you learned to play because the original response was too much, too loud, too strange. Every "you should" that arrived before you were old enough to question it. By the time most people reach thirty, they've been overwritten so many times they can't feel the original underneath. They call it maturity. They call it fitting in. They call the weight of other people's handwriting — themselves. Psychologists have a name for this: the accumulated self. The version of you built not from the inside out, but from the outside in. The most dangerous thing about it is how comfortable it becomes.

> **SLIDE:** *Single word: COMFORTABLE. Slightly blurred, as if seen through rain-streaked glass.*

---

**03 — THE SURPRISE [1:10 – 1:50]**

Here's the part most people miss. You are not trying to find yourself. You already know. The question isn't discovery — it's subtraction. Every self-help industry in the world is built on addition. Add a habit. Add a framework. Add a morning routine. But the original isn't gone. It's underneath everything you added. The researchers didn't write new text to recover Archimedes. They used light to see what was already there. You don't need more self-work. You need less performance. The self isn't missing. It's buried.

> **SLIDE:** *Large white text on black: "You don't need to find yourself. You need to stop covering yourself up."*

---

**04 — THE RUSH OF DOING NOTHING [1:50 – 2:20]**

Every instinct will tell you to move. To optimise. To journal your way to authenticity, track your feelings, run the diagnostic. But what if the bravest thing is to stop adding? You already know which rooms you come alive in. You already know which conversations make you want to leave your own body. You already know. You've known for years. You just haven't been allowed to trust it. Sit with that. Not as a technique. Not as a practice. Just sit. That discomfort — that is the original bleeding through.

> **SLIDE:** *Black screen. Silence. Hold for two beats.*

---

**05 — THE PATH [2:20 – 2:50]**

Three moves. That's all. First: notice what makes you forget time. Not what you're good at. What makes you forget. Second: notice what tightens in your chest when someone tells you to want something. That tightening is your body disagreeing before your brain can stop it. Third: choose one room in your life — one conversation, one decision — where you stop performing. Not forever. Just once. And see what comes through.

> **SLIDE:** *Three lines of plain white text on black: "Forget time. / Trust the tightening. / Stop performing." No bullet points. Wide line spacing.*

---

**06 — THE CLOSE [2:50 – 3:00]**

The monks needed the parchment. They weren't trying to erase Archimedes. They just had prayers to write and no vellum to spare. The people who overwrote you weren't villains. They just had needs, and you were available. But Archimedes is still there. And so are you. All you need is the right light.

> **CLOSING LINE:** *"All you need is the right light."*
