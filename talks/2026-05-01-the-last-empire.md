## THE LAST EMPIRE

*What Ray Dalio's ledgers say about the world you're about to inherit*

**Theme:** philosophy

**Source idea:** The New World Order using Ray Dalio's principles — how all shifts are moving toward state control and data, and the only solution when AI takes everyone's jobs is for government to step in with universal funding during the transition.

---

**01 — THE HOOK [0:00 – 0:35]**

In 1637, a single tulip bulb in Amsterdam sold for more than a skilled craftsman earned in 10 years. Three months later, the whole thing collapsed overnight. The craftsman kept his hands. The bulb became compost.

Ray Dalio spent 40 years charting these moments. Not tulips. Empires. The Dutch, the British, the American, all bent the same way: up on productivity, cracking on debt, splitting on inequality, gone on conflict. He called it the Big Cycle. Said it runs about 250 years. Said America's started in 1776.

You can do that math in your head.

> **SLIDE:** *A single faded tulip against black. No text.*

---

**02 — THE PAIN POINT [0:35 – 1:10]**

Here's what a late-cycle empire feels like from the inside. The wealth gap reaches a number that can't be justified out loud anymore. The middle class clocks in harder and takes home less. The institutions that felt permanent — currencies, governments, the quiet agreement that the system basically works — start feeling like something everyone is backing away from in small, polite steps.

And we're dropping AI into all of that.

Between 2025 and 2035, an estimated 300 million jobs get automated. The pattern Dalio pulled from every collapsing empire is the same: when a society produces more than it can distribute, it breaks. Every time.

We've been here before. Just never at this speed.

> **SLIDE:** *"300,000,000" in white on black. Below it, grey: "jobs. not a forecast. a function."*

---

**03 — THE SURPRISE [1:10 – 1:50]**

Strip the AI conversation down to its studs and what you find is a fight over territory.

Data is territory. And territory, across all of human history, has always ended up governed. Land. Coal. Capital. Private hands held each one first. The state came in later, every time, because concentrated power in an ungoverned system eventually lands on everyone's doorstep.

The only institution with enough surface area to catch 300 million people at once is the state.

A governance calculation. Same math, different century.

> **SLIDE:** *The world's undersea internet cable map. Bright lines on black ocean. No labels.*

---

**04 — THE RUSH OF DOING NOTHING [1:50 – 2:20]**

Every instinct says move. Build faster. Regulate after. Let the market find the floor.

Dalio has a name for that. The late-cycle reflex. The moment when decline is visible and the people with the most to protect argue loudest for doing nothing, because doing nothing holds their position one more quarter.

But nobody knows what comes after this. I don't. You don't. The people building the systems don't either, whatever they say on stage.

Sitting with that, without reaching for a framework or shipping a solution, is probably the most honest place you can stand right now.

The tulip teaches you something: you can be living inside an agreement and not know it until it ends.

> **SLIDE:** *Black. No text. Hold 3 seconds.*

---

**05 — THE PATH [2:20 – 2:50]**

2 things governments need to do, and 1 demand worth making.

Governments need to treat UBI as transition infrastructure, the same way they laid interstates for the car economy. Roads weren't charity. They were the architecture of what came next.

Companies deploying AI at scale get taxed on the labour they displace, proportionally. The machine takes the job; the machine funds the crossing.

And you need to decide what you think the data economy owes you. Privacy as an opt-out has already lost. Ownership is the ask.

The new order gets built either way. The only open question is whether it gets built around you or with you.

> **SLIDE:** *INFRASTRUCTURE. PROPORTION. OWNERSHIP. Stacked. White on dark grey.*

---

**06 — THE CLOSE [2:50 – 3:00]**

In 1637, the tulip was worth everything. Then nothing. The craftsman with his hands kept working.

Whatever gets built next gets built by whoever still has something real to trade when the agreement collapses.

Make sure that's you. Make sure that's everyone.

> **SLIDE:** *The tulip again. This time, it's growing.*

> **CLOSING LINE:** *"Make sure that's you. Make sure that's everyone."*
