# The Guillotine We Built

*An Enlightenment man, a falling blade, and the phone in your pocket.*

**Theme:** philosophy

**Source idea:** At the end of the eighteenth century, the Enlightenment promised the triumph of reason. Superstition, religious dogmas and absolute rulers would no longer obscure or corrupt reality, and truth would bring freedom to all. But three centuries later, truth seems further away than ever. We aren't free, but increasingly entangled in opaque technological structures. Unknown forms of intelligence threaten to turn arms in the hands of new corrupt leaders. False prophets present themselves in our frantic quest for meaning. Have the promises of the Enlightenment turned against us? Did we ourselves bring to power the excesses of the rational? Where do we find vision in this confused world?

**01 — THE HOOK [0:00 – 0:35]**

Paris. 1789. A doctor named Joseph-Ignace Guillotin walks into the National Assembly with a folded sketch in his pocket.

He's a gentle man. A reformer. He's spent most of his life trying to make the world a touch less cruel.

So he's drawn a machine. Painless. Equal. Scientific. A blade that falls clean.

Four years later, his machine has shortened 17,000 men and women, most of them friends of friends, and a fair number of the people who clapped when he first held up his sketch.

Joseph-Ignace lived to see it. He died in 1814, in his bed. His grandchildren had to change the family name. The sound of it had grown too heavy to carry.

**02 — THE PAIN POINT [0:35 – 1:10]**

We're still living inside Joseph's sketch.

Painless. Equal. Scientific. Rational. An algorithm decided if you got the loan. Another one quietly tossed your daughter's résumé out of the pile last Tuesday. A third one chose what you saw the second your eyes opened this morning.

And here you sit. In the freest century in human history. Tired. Foggy. Vaguely sure something's been done to you but unable to point at the doer.

You can't sue the algorithm. You can't vote it out. You can't even read it. It's the perfect ruler. It doesn't know your name.

**03 — THE SURPRISE [1:10 – 1:50]**

Here's the part that ought to keep you up.

The Enlightenment worked.

Every promise it made came in. Universal literacy. Vaccines. Constitutional democracy. A device in your pocket that holds every poem ever written and a fair amount of the pornography.

And the same patient reason that beat smallpox engineered the slot machine your 14-year-old falls asleep with. The same reason that ended kings invented shareholders. The same reason that freed your speech sold your attention to the highest bidder while you were typing.

Reason was tyranny's foreign exchange student. We thought we were teaching it our values. It was taking notes.

**04 — THE RUSH OF DOING NOTHING [1:50 – 2:20]**

You want a plan.

I can feel you wanting it from up here. Your hand is already drifting toward your phone to make a note. Five steps. A framework you can post about Tuesday.

Put the phone down.

That itch you're feeling? That hunger for a clean answer? That's reason offering to fix what reason broke. The snake selling you the apple twice.

Sit with not knowing. Sit with the possibility that the smartest people on earth haven't worked this one out, and won't by Q4. Let the room go quiet. Let it be awkward. Let your foot fall asleep.

> **SLIDE:** *Black screen. No movement. Hold.*

**05 — THE PATH [2:20 – 2:50]**

Three small things. Embarrassingly small.

Read a book older than the printing press. Anything. Marcus Aurelius. Ecclesiastes. The Tao Te Ching. Take your pick. Just make sure a human needed it before a machine ranked it.

Once a week, sit across from a person you disagree with. In a room. No screen between you. Let it be uncomfortable. That's the point.

And every day, do one thing nobody can measure. Stare at a tree. Pray if you pray. Walk without counting your steps.

That's the whole prescription. Nobody's going to sell it back to you in a webinar.

**06 — THE CLOSE [2:50 – 3:00]**

Joseph-Ignace died horrified at what his kind, reasonable little machine had become.

Ours fits in a pocket. The blade keeps falling. It just doesn't make a sound anymore.

Step out from under it.
